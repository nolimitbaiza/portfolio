Perfect hindsight: the benchmark that keeps a trading bot honest.

My agent earns €2.54M a year trading a battery in Germany. Is that good? The number alone can't tell you. Here's the yardstick I use instead.

Reading time
4 min
Data
Real 2025 prices, full year
Project
AI Grid Trader

Profit says more about the market than the bot

Run the same agent with the same battery through 2025 and it earns $0.87M in California, €2.54M in Germany and A$8.18M in South Australia. South Australia's prices swing far harder, so there is simply more money on the table. A good strategy in a calm market can earn less than a poor one in a wild market, and the currencies don't even match.

The ceiling: perfect hindsight

Perfect hindsight is the same optimiser, run once over the whole year with the prices that actually happened. It knows every price in advance, so no real strategy can beat it with the same battery and the same accounting. It isn't a target; it's a ruler. I report the agent as a share of it:

2025, full year, after battery wear

MarketPerfect hindsightAI agentShare
California$0.92M$0.87M95%
Germany€2.68M€2.54M95%
South AustraliaA$8.75MA$8.18M93%

Now the markets compare directly: in each one the agent captures 93 to 95% of what was possible, while only ever seeing a forecast.

And the floor

A ceiling on its own can still flatter, so I also compare against something simple: a rule that buys when the price is below its 3-hour average and sells when it's above. Over 2025, after wear, it loses $0.36M in California and €0.55M in Germany, and earns A$1.33M in South Australia, 15% of the ceiling. If a strategy can't clearly beat that, its cleverness isn't adding anything.

What a worse forecast costs

The agent never sees the future. Every hour it gets a fresh 24-hour forecast, re-plans, and acts on the next hour only. The simulated forecast misses short spikes and flattens peaks further ahead, and the demo has a slider for how wrong it is. Here's the share of hindsight as the forecast gets worse:

Share of perfect hindsight by forecast error, 2025

Forecast errorCaliforniaGermanySouth Australia
±5%98%99%98%
±10%97%97%95%
±20% demo default95%95%93%
±40%89%86%89%

Even with twice the default error, the agent keeps 86 to 89%. Re-planning every hour means a bad guess gets corrected before it compounds. The gap is also a price tag on forecasting: in Germany, going from ±20% to ±5% is worth about €100,000 a year for this one battery. That's what a better forecast model would need to earn its keep.

Ask for both numbers

A trading result on its own is hard to judge. With a ceiling it becomes a percentage you can compare across markets and years, and with a floor you can see whether the strategy is doing anything a simple rule couldn't. When I see a result quoted without either, those are the first two questions I ask.

Numbers come from AI Grid Trader's engine with the demo's default battery for each market (100 MWh, 25 MW, 88% round trip), a ±20% forecast error unless stated, and 2025 prices. In California and Germany a fifth of the power is held back for grid services. To reproduce them, run node tools/writing_numbers.js in the project repository (opens in a new tab).
